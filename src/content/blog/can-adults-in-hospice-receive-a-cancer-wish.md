Yes, adults in hospice may be able to receive a meaningful cancer wish or Moment, depending on timing, comfort, health, logistics, family needs, and what is possible.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. A hospice cancer wish for adults may look different than the original idea, but the heart of the Moment can still remain: time, dignity, connection, and the people who matter most.

Hospice-related requests can carry urgency. Comfort and energy may change quickly, and plans may need to shift with care. One More Moment reviews each request thoughtfully and does not provide medical advice, medical care, emergency services, or a guarantee that every urgent hospice request can be fulfilled.

For a broader overview of adult cancer wishes in Michigan, visit our guide to [wish granting for adults with cancer in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## Hospice Does Not Mean the Moment Has to Stop

When someone enters hospice, a family may wonder whether an adult cancer wish in hospice is still possible. In some situations, it may be.

The original wish may need to change. A trip may become a local gathering. A restaurant visit may become a special dinner at home. A larger event may become a quiet visit with a few loved ones. The details may shift, but the meaning can still remain.

Hospice does not erase the person, their relationships, their memories, or what they hoped to experience. It may simply mean the Moment needs to be shaped around comfort, timing, privacy, and what the person and family can realistically manage.

## When Travel Is No Longer Possible

Travel is not always possible during hospice care. A person may not have the energy for a long drive, a hotel stay, a public event, or time away from the support they need.

When travel is no longer possible, a Moment may sometimes become something more local, private, or brought to the person instead. A favorite place may be represented through food, music, photos, a meaningful object, or people gathering nearby. A planned outing may become an at-home experience that is easier on the person and family.

This kind of change can be tender. Families may feel disappointed that the original plan cannot happen. But an adapted Moment can still create connection, comfort, and memory.

## What Can a Hospice Moment Look Like?

A hospice wish for adults does not have to be large or public. Often, the most meaningful requests are simple, personal, and centered on the people closest to the person facing cancer.

A hospice-related Moment may include:

- A favorite meal delivered
- A meaningful gift box or care package
- A family gathering
- A favorite place brought to the person
- A quiet visit with loved ones
- A special dinner at home
- A video message or personal note if available

These examples are possibilities, not promises. Access to specific people, messages, items, locations, vendors, or timing may depend on availability and what can be coordinated quickly and responsibly.

An end of life cancer wish may be quiet. It may involve a few people, a familiar room, favorite food, music, prayer, laughter, or a chance to say thank you. The size of the Moment is not what gives it meaning.

## Why Timing Matters

Timing matters for every Moment, and it can matter even more when hospice care is involved.

Hospice-related requests may require quick decisions because:

- Urgent requests may require quick decisions
- Comfort and energy can change quickly
- Family schedules and hospice care needs matter
- Earlier requests are often easier to plan

Earlier requests may allow more time to understand the person's wishes, coordinate loved ones, plan around hospice care needs, arrange meals or vendors, consider privacy, and adapt if health or comfort changes.

One More Moment understands that families do not always know when to reach out. Sometimes the need becomes clear only when time feels short. If a request is urgent, the team may still review what is possible, but timing and logistics can affect what can be done.

## Who Can Help Start the Request?

The person facing cancer may begin the request, but they do not have to carry the process alone.

A hospice-related Moment request may be started by:

- The person facing cancer
- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A chaplain
- A church or community contact

The person facing cancer should remain central to the request whenever possible. One More Moment wants to understand what would feel meaningful to them, who should be involved, and what would support dignity and comfort.

To begin, visit [Request a Moment](/moments/#request-a-moment). If you are not sure whether someone qualifies, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## Dignity and Privacy Come First

One More Moment handles hospice-related requests with care, dignity, and respect.

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many remain private. A person and family do not need to share photos, details, medical information, or personal moments publicly for the experience to matter.

Privacy can be especially important during hospice care. Families may be managing grief, visitors, medical equipment, fatigue, spiritual needs, or tender conversations. One More Moment respects that every family has different boundaries.

For more information about how personal information is handled, review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/).

## Thoughtful Review and Donor Stewardship

Urgent requests are reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity, and every donor deserves to know their support is used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the meaning of the requested Moment, timing, feasibility, available resources, comfort, privacy, safety, and mission fit. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability. It gives the team a chance to understand the person, the family, and what may be possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can adults in hospice receive a cancer wish?

Yes. Adults in hospice may be able to receive a meaningful cancer wish or Moment, depending on timing, comfort, health, logistics, family needs, available resources, and what is possible.

### What if the original wish is no longer possible?

If the original wish is no longer possible, the Moment may need to change. A trip may become a local gathering, a restaurant visit may become a special dinner at home, or a public outing may become a private visit with loved ones.

### Can a Moment be brought to the person instead?

Yes, when feasible. A favorite meal, meaningful gift box, care package, family gathering, favorite place represented at home, video message, personal note, or other adapted experience may help bring the Moment closer to the person.

### Who can request a hospice Moment?

The person facing cancer, spouse or partner, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, chaplain, church contact, or community contact may help start a request.

### Are hospice Moments shared publicly?

Only with permission. Some Moments are shared publicly, and many remain private. A Moment does not need to be posted, photographed, or publicly told in order to matter.

### How can someone request an adult cancer wish in Michigan?

Start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment). If you have questions first, email [contactus@onemoremoment.org](mailto:contactus@onemoremoment.org).
