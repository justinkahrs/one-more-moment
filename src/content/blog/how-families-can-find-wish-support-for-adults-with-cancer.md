Families looking for wish support for adults with cancer may be able to find help through independent nonprofit organizations that serve adults, especially when the person they love is facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones. A Moment may be a family trip, a favorite meal, a sporting event, a quiet gathering, or something simple brought to the home when travel is not possible.

One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. Families often search phrases like "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer," "adult cancer wish," or "wish support for adults with cancer" because they are trying to find help for someone they love. This guide explains where to begin and what information may help.

For a broader overview, visit our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/). If you already know someone who may need support, you can start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).

## Why Finding Adult Wish Support Can Be Confusing

Most people are more familiar with wish programs for children. When someone they love is an adult facing advanced cancer, families may not know what words to use, which organizations serve adults, or whether adult cancer wishes are even possible.

That confusion is understandable. Families may be trying to help quickly while also navigating health updates, caregiving, travel limits, family schedules, and emotions that are hard to put into words.

The important thing to know is that adult wish support may exist through independent organizations with their own missions, locations, eligibility guidelines, and review processes. In Michigan, One More Moment focuses specifically on adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

## Search Terms Families May Use

Families often search different phrases while looking for the same kind of help. Some may search for "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer" because that is the wish-granting name they know. Others may search for an "adult cancer wish," a "wish program for adults with cancer," a "Stage 4 cancer wish," or a "cancer wish program in Michigan."

Those searches usually come from love and urgency. A spouse, adult child, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, social worker, church contact, or community member may be trying to find a way to create meaningful time for someone they care about.

If you are still trying to understand whether adults can receive wish support, read [Do Adults With Cancer Get a Wish?](/blog/do-adults-with-cancer-get-a-wish/).

## What One More Moment Does

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

We use the word "Moment" because the experience does not have to look one specific way. Some Moments are active and joyful. Some are quiet and private. Some involve travel or community partners. Others happen close to home with the people who matter most.

Each request is reviewed with care. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability, but it gives One More Moment the chance to understand the person, the situation, and what kind of Moment may be possible.

If you are trying to understand eligibility, visit [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## What Kind of Support May Be Possible

Adult cancer wishes can take many forms. The most meaningful Moment is not always the largest one. Often, it is the one that reflects the person facing cancer and gives their family time together.

A Moment may include:

- A family trip or weekend away
- A favorite meal
- A sporting event
- A family gathering
- A meaningful local experience
- Quiet time with loved ones
- Something brought to the home if travel is not possible

Health, timing, accessibility, available resources, and the details of the request can all shape what may be possible. One More Moment reviews each situation individually and with care.

## Who Can Start the Search or Request

The adult facing cancer can reach out directly, but they do not have to begin the process alone.

People who may help start the search or request include:

- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A church contact
- A community member

Whenever possible, the person facing cancer should remain central to the request. Their comfort, preferences, privacy, dignity, and consent matter.

If you are wondering whether a caregiver or loved one can begin, read [Can Caregivers Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/can-caregivers-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/).

## What Information Helps

You do not need every detail figured out before reaching out. Still, a few details can help One More Moment better understand the request and the timing.

Helpful information may include:

- The person's diagnosis or general cancer status
- Where they live in Michigan
- Timing concerns or important dates
- Travel ability and accessibility needs
- Family or caregiver contacts
- What would be meaningful to the person facing cancer
- Whether the Moment can be shared publicly or should remain private

If you want a more detailed overview, read [What Information Is Needed to Request an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## Why Families Should Not Wait for a Perfect Plan

A request does not have to be fully developed before someone reaches out. Families sometimes wait because they are not sure whether the idea is big enough, specific enough, or possible enough.

Starting the conversation can be enough. You can share what you know, explain what would feel meaningful, and let One More Moment review the request with care.

Timing can matter. Health, comfort, mobility, family availability, weather, and logistics may affect what is possible. If a Moment matters to someone you love, reaching out sooner can give the team more room to understand the request.

## Privacy and Dignity

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Receiving a Moment does not require someone to share photos, personal details, or medical information publicly. Families should share personal and medical information carefully. One More Moment uses submitted information to review eligibility and coordinate requests. You can review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information.

## How to Request a Moment

If you know an adult in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer and you are looking for wish support, start by submitting a request or contacting One More Moment.

You do not need perfect words. You do not need every date, cost, or detail finalized before beginning. You can start with what you know and what would be meaningful.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How can families find wish support for an adult with cancer?

Families can look for independent adult wish-granting nonprofits that serve adults with cancer. In Michigan, One More Moment helps adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

### Is One More Moment affiliated with Make-A-Wish?

No. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit with its own mission, eligibility guidelines, review process, and focus on adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

### What kind of adult cancer wish may be possible?

A Moment may include a family trip or weekend away, favorite meal, sporting event, family gathering, meaningful local experience, quiet time with loved ones, or something brought to the home if travel is not possible.

### Who can help request a Moment?

The adult facing cancer, a spouse, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, social worker, church contact, or community member can help begin the request.

### What if we are not sure someone qualifies?

It is still okay to ask. One More Moment reviews requests with care. You can read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/) or begin with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).
