Families often know they want to request a wish for someone they love, but they may not know what kinds of wishes are possible. They may wonder what types of wishes can adults request, whether the idea is too simple, or whether an adult cancer wish has to look a certain way.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. A Moment may be joyful, quiet, familiar, practical, emotional, or playful. What matters most is that it reflects the person facing cancer and the people who matter to them.

Many people search for phrases like "wish for adults with cancer" or "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer" when they are trying to find help. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit and is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish.

This article offers examples of the types of wishes adults with cancer can request. These are starting points, not promises or medical guidance. Every request is reviewed thoughtfully based on mission fit, timing, feasibility, available resources, safety, dignity, and the details of the Moment being requested.

For a broader overview of adult cancer wishes in Michigan, visit our guide to [wish granting for adults with cancer in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## A Moment Should Reflect the Person

Adult cancer wishes are not one-size-fits-all.

One person may want a weekend away with family. Another may want a favorite meal, a visit with grandchildren, a sporting event, a quiet day at home, or a chance to thank the people who helped during treatment. A Stage IV cancer wish, advanced cancer wish, or metastatic cancer wish does not need to be large to be meaningful.

The best adult cancer wish ideas usually begin with simple questions:

- Who does the person want beside them?
- Where do they feel most comfortable or most like themselves?
- What memory, place, food, activity, or relationship carries meaning?
- What would help remove stress so the family can simply be together?

The answer may lead to something public and energetic, or something private and peaceful. Both can fit the heart of a Moment.

## Family and Connection Wishes

Many adult cancer wishes in Michigan are centered on family, friendship, and time together.

A family and connection Moment may include:

- Dinner with loved ones
- Time with children or grandchildren
- Gathering close friends
- Seeing someone important
- Saying thank you to people who helped during treatment

Sometimes the request is about bringing people together in one place. Sometimes it is about making a visit easier, creating space for conversation, or helping a person share time with the people who have walked with them through cancer.

These Moments may be simple, but they can carry deep meaning. A shared meal, a living room full of familiar voices, or a visit from someone important can become the kind of memory a family holds close.

## Michigan Getaway Wishes

Some people want a change of scenery without leaving Michigan. A local getaway can offer time away from appointments, routines, and the daily weight of cancer.

A Michigan getaway Moment may include:

- A weekend in Glen Arbor
- A trip to Frankenmuth
- A cottage or lakeside stay
- A peaceful place near the water
- A favorite Michigan town

For some families, the location matters because it is tied to childhood, anniversaries, vacations, or family traditions. For others, the wish may simply be time near water, a quiet place to rest, or a chance to sit together somewhere beautiful.

Earlier requests are often easier to plan for travel-based Moments because lodging, family schedules, transportation, comfort needs, privacy needs, and local support may all need coordination.

## Food and Restaurant Wishes

Food can hold memory in a powerful way. A favorite restaurant, a familiar recipe, or a meal connected to a place or season can help a family feel close to a person and their story.

A food or restaurant Moment may include:

- A favorite restaurant
- A private dinner
- A chef-prepared meal
- Breakfast or lunch with loved ones
- Meaningful food tied to a place or memory

Some food wishes are about celebration. Others are about comfort, tradition, or revisiting a place that has been part of the family's life. The setting does not have to be elaborate. What matters is the meaning behind the meal and the people gathered around it.

## Event and Experience Wishes

Some Moments are built around an event, activity, or experience that the person has always loved or wants to share again.

An event or experience Moment may include:

- A Detroit Tigers game
- A Detroit Lions game
- A concert
- A local show
- A museum
- A scenic boat ride
- Mini golf
- A shopping day

These requests may involve more planning, especially when tickets, transportation, accessibility, seating, weather, timing, or crowd size matter. One More Moment reviews each request carefully to understand what may be possible and what support would help the person and family experience the Moment with dignity and comfort.

## Quiet or Private Wishes

Not every Moment needs to involve travel, tickets, or a public setting. Some people want privacy, calm, and familiar surroundings.

A quiet or private Moment may include:

- A quiet day together
- A favorite place brought to the person
- A private family gathering
- A simple experience at home when travel is not possible

When cancer changes energy, mobility, or comfort, a private Moment may be the most fitting option. A favorite meal at home, music in the backyard, a small gathering, or a meaningful object or place brought closer can still create connection.

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private. A person and family do not have to make their experience public for it to matter.

## What If the Original Wish Has to Change?

Cancer can change plans quickly. Energy, symptoms, appointments, weather, transportation, and family availability can all affect what is realistic.

When needed, One More Moment may adjust a Moment so it still reflects the person while respecting comfort, dignity, timing, and safety. A planned trip might become a local experience. A public outing might become a private gathering. A restaurant dinner might become a meal brought home.

Changing the plan does not mean the Moment has failed. It means the request is being handled with care for the person and the people who love them.

## Who Can Help Start a Request?

The person facing cancer may begin a request, but they do not have to carry the process alone.

A Moment request or referral may be started by:

- The person facing cancer
- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A chaplain
- A church or community contact

The person facing cancer should remain central to the request. One More Moment wants to understand what would feel meaningful to them, who should be involved, and what support may be needed.

To begin, visit [Request a Moment](/moments/#request-a-moment). If you are unsure whether someone qualifies, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## Why Timing Matters

Earlier requests are often easier to plan because they allow time for coordination, family schedules, travel, meals, lodging, comfort needs, privacy needs, and local support.

That extra time can matter for adult cancer wishes in Michigan, especially when a Moment involves multiple people, transportation, lodging, accessibility needs, a venue, tickets, or coordination with caregivers and care teams.

One More Moment understands that families do not always have the benefit of time. Some requests are urgent. Still, when possible, starting earlier gives the team more room to understand the request and explore what may be feasible.

## Dignity, Privacy, and Public Sharing

Dignity and privacy are part of the process.

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private. A family should not feel pressured to turn a personal experience into a public story.

Personal and medical information submitted through a Moment request is used to review eligibility and coordinate the request. For more details, review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/).

## Thoughtful Review and Donor Stewardship

Each request is reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity, and every donor deserves to know their support is used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the meaning of the requested Moment, timing, logistics, available resources, safety, privacy, mission fit, and whether the request can be supported well. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability. It gives the team a chance to understand the person, the family, and what may be possible.

Because corporate donors and generous families and supporters help cover overhead, 100% of public donations go directly toward wish granting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What types of wishes can adults with cancer request?

Adults with cancer may request meaningful Moments centered on time with loved ones. Examples include family dinners, visits with children or grandchildren, Michigan getaways, favorite restaurants, sporting events, concerts, local experiences, quiet private gatherings, or simple experiences at home. Each request is reviewed individually.

### Can an adult cancer wish be a local Michigan experience?

Yes. A local Michigan experience may be a good fit. Examples include a weekend in Glen Arbor, a trip to Frankenmuth, a cottage or lakeside stay, a favorite Michigan town, a restaurant, a museum, or another place connected to the person's life and memories.

### Can an adult cancer wish include a sporting event?

Yes, a sporting event may be requested. Examples might include a Detroit Tigers game, Detroit Lions game, or another meaningful local event. Approval depends on timing, feasibility, tickets, accessibility, safety, available resources, and the details of the request.

### Can a wish be private?

Yes. Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission, and many stay private. A Moment does not need to be photographed, posted, or publicly shared to be meaningful.

### What happens if the original wish has to change?

Cancer can change plans quickly. If the original wish has to change, One More Moment may adjust the Moment when possible so it still reflects the person while respecting comfort, dignity, timing, and safety.

### How can someone request an adult cancer wish in Michigan?

Start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment). The person facing cancer, a spouse or partner, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, chaplain, or community contact may help begin the request.

## About One More Moment
