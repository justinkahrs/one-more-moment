Yes. Adults with cancer who have children may request a Moment through One More Moment if they meet the organization's eligibility guidelines.

Many families search for whether adults with cancer, especially parents, can receive a wish. Some search for "make a wish for adults with cancer who have children" because Make-A-Wish is the name they already know. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

For a broader overview, visit our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## Why This Question Matters

When a parent is facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, the wish is often not just about the adult.

It may be about time with their children. It may be about comfort, memory-making, connection, and a chance to share something meaningful without the family having to carry every practical detail alone.

For some parents, a Moment may be joyful and active. For others, it may be quiet and close to home. The size of the Moment is not what gives it meaning. What matters is that it reflects the person facing cancer and the people they love.

## What Kind of Moment Might a Parent Request?

An adult cancer wish centered on children can take many forms. It does not need to be elaborate, public, or expensive to matter.

A parent might request:

- A weekend away with children
- A quiet family dinner
- A lake or beach day
- A favorite sporting event
- A small family gathering
- A special activity the parent and child have always wanted to do together
- Something brought to the home if travel is not possible

These are examples, not promises. Every request is reviewed with care based on eligibility, timing, logistics, safety, available resources, and whether the request fits One More Moment's mission.

## Does the Child Have to Be the Person Receiving the Wish?

No. One More Moment serves the adult facing cancer.

But the Moment can absolutely be centered around time with children, grandchildren, or loved ones. A parent may be the person who qualifies, while the experience itself is about sharing time with their family.

That distinction matters. The goal is not to create a children's wish through an adult program. The goal is to help an adult facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer experience a meaningful Moment with the people who matter most.

## Who Can Help Start the Request?

A request may begin with the adult facing cancer, or someone close to them may help start the conversation.

People who may help begin a request include:

- Spouse or partner
- Adult child
- Family member
- Friend
- Caregiver
- Hospice worker
- Oncology team member
- Nurse navigator
- Social worker
- Chaplain
- Church or community contact

The person facing cancer should remain at the center of the request whenever possible. A loved one or care professional can help with the first message, but the Moment should still reflect the person's comfort, dignity, preferences, and privacy.

If you are ready to begin, you can use the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).

## Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?

One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan who are facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

Eligibility may also include Michigan residency, financial or logistical need, the nature of the requested Moment, timing, available resources, and confirmation from an oncology provider.

If you are unsure whether someone may qualify, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/). You can also contact One More Moment before submitting a request if you need help understanding whether a situation may fit.

## Privacy and Dignity

One More Moment understands that families facing advanced cancer may be navigating private, emotional, and complicated circumstances.

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Receiving a Moment does not require a family to share photos, medical details, or personal information publicly. The focus is on honoring the person and family with care.

## How to Request a Moment

If you know an adult in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer who may benefit from a meaningful Moment with their children or loved ones, you can start by submitting a request or contacting One More Moment.

Use the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment) to begin. If you are still learning what may be possible, you can also explore [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/) or review [who qualifies](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

If you would like to help make future Moments possible for Michigan families, you can [donate to One More Moment](/donate/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can adults with cancer who have children request a wish?

Yes. Adults with cancer who have children may request a Moment through One More Moment if they meet the organization's eligibility guidelines. One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

### Is One More Moment the same as Make-A-Wish?

No. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. Families often search for "Make-A-Wish for adults," but One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

### Can the Moment be focused on time with children?

Yes. One More Moment serves the adult facing cancer, but the Moment can be centered around time with children, grandchildren, family members, or other loved ones.

### Can someone else request a Moment for a parent with cancer?

Yes. A spouse, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, social worker, or community contact may help begin the request.

### What if the parent cannot travel?

Some Moments can be planned close to home or brought to the home when travel is not possible. One More Moment reviews each request based on the person's needs, comfort, timing, safety, logistics, and available resources.
