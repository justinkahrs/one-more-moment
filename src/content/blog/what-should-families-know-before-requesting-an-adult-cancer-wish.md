Families do not need to have a perfect plan before requesting an adult cancer wish, but knowing a few basics about eligibility, timing, location, guests, accessibility, and flexibility can make the process easier.

You may have a specific idea, or you may only know that someone you love wants meaningful time with family. Either can be a starting point. An adult cancer wish request begins a conversation about the person, what matters to them, and what may be possible.

One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. Our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/) offers an overview of our work.

## Start With the Basics of Eligibility

One More Moment generally serves adults who live in Michigan and face Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer. A person may qualify when they want a meaningful Moment with loved ones and need support because cost, timing, logistics, health, or available resources make it difficult to create alone.

Confirmation from an oncology provider may be requested as part of reviewing eligibility. Every request is considered individually, and submitting a request does not guarantee approval or funding.

If you are unsure about cancer wish eligibility, you do not have to resolve every question yourself before reaching out. Read [who qualifies for a One More Moment wish](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/) and share the general situation through the request process.

Families sometimes find us while searching for a "wish program for adults with cancer," a "Stage 4 cancer wish," or "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer." One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. We have our own mission, eligibility guidelines, and review process.

## Meaningful Moments Can Happen Close to Home

One More Moment primarily focuses on creating Moments within Michigan. Staying local can make it easier to coordinate volunteers, local businesses, transportation, accessibility, meals, lodging, and timing while making thoughtful use of available resources.

That leaves room for many personal experiences: a favorite meal, a family outing, a Michigan getaway, a sporting event, a gathering, or time at a place that matters to the person. When feasible, something meaningful may be brought to the person if travel is difficult.

A Moment does not need to involve a distant destination to matter. For more ideas, read [Do Adult Cancer Wishes Have to Involve Travel?](/blog/do-adult-cancer-wishes-have-to-involve-travel/).

## You Do Not Need Every Detail Planned

You do not need every reservation, location, date, or activity decided before contacting One More Moment. Start with the heart of the idea:

- What would feel meaningful?
- Why does it matter to the person?
- Who would they like beside them?
- Are there timing concerns?
- What accessibility or mobility needs should guide planning?

Someone might want a favorite dinner with their children, a quiet weekend with a spouse, or a chance to revisit a familiar place. Explaining why the idea matters can help preserve its meaning if the details need to change.

Let the person facing cancer guide the conversation whenever possible. Their preferences, comfort, and consent matter more than creating an elaborate surprise.

## Reach Out Before the Perfect Time Arrives

Health, treatment schedules, mobility, energy, travel ability, and availability can change quickly. Reaching out sooner may allow more flexibility for family schedules, accessible options, transportation, and a backup plan.

You do not have to wait until treatment ends or every family member has agreed on a date to ask about support. Share what you know now, including any urgency or uncertainty.

If time is short, say so early. One More Moment can consider what may be feasible, though a particular date or experience cannot be promised. Read [what to do when an adult cancer wish needs to happen quickly](/blog/what-to-do-when-an-adult-cancer-wish-needs-to-happen-quickly/).

## The Final Moment May Look Different From the First Idea

Every request receives a thoughtful review, but not every request can be fulfilled exactly as proposed. Cost, availability, accessibility, timing, geography, health considerations, donor support, and logistics can all affect what is possible.

This is helpful context for understanding how adult cancer wish programs work. Sharing a request gives the organization a chance to consider the idea and ask questions. It is not a confirmation that reservations, tickets, travel, or funding have been approved.

Our article [Does Every Adult Cancer Wish Get Granted?](/blog/does-every-adult-cancer-wish-get-granted/) explains more about the review process.

Flexibility can make room for an experience that still holds the original meaning. For example:

- A long trip may become a Michigan getaway.
- A large event may become a smaller experience with the people closest to the person.
- Travel may become something brought to the person, when feasible.
- A difficult-to-access experience may become another option centered on the same interest or connection.

These are possibilities, not promises. Tell the team which parts matter most and which details could change. The goal is to understand the person behind the request.

## Think About Who the Person Wants to Include

One More Moment's current guest guidelines provide funding for the cancer warrior and up to three family members or friends of the warrior's choice. Additional individuals are welcome to join if space allows, but they are responsible for their own expenses.

It helps to share the hoped-for group size early. Larger groups can add costs and create challenges with lodging, transportation, tickets, space, and accessibility. Knowing who the person most wants beside them helps the team understand the request and discuss practical options.

Families do not need a final guest list before reaching out. Start with the person's wishes, explain any important family considerations, and be open about which arrangements may need discussion.

## Share Accessibility and Comfort Needs Early

Practical details help shape an experience around the person. Let One More Moment know about needs or preferences such as:

- Wheelchair use or mobility limitations
- Transportation needs
- Concerns about crowds or busy settings
- Rest breaks or a shorter outing
- Travel limitations
- Other accessibility or comfort considerations relevant to planning

A quiet venue, an accessible entrance, a nearby location, or time to rest may make a difference in planning. Sharing these details early helps the team explore realistic options without asking the family to have every solution ready.

For medical questions about activities, travel, or treatment timing, consult the person's care team. One More Moment does not provide medical advice. A Moment should fit around care rather than require changes to it.

## Someone Can Help Start the Request

The person facing cancer may submit a request, but they do not need to manage the process alone. A spouse, adult child, other family member, friend, caregiver, social worker, oncology team member, hospice professional, or church or community contact may help begin the conversation.

Choose a contact person the individual trusts and wants involved. That person can help with messages, practical details, and updates while keeping the wishes of the person facing cancer at the center.

## What Information Should Families Have Ready?

A few details can make the first conversation easier:

- The person's name and the best contact information
- General cancer status relevant to eligibility
- Where the person lives in Michigan
- What would be meaningful and why
- Timing concerns or urgency
- Accessibility, mobility, or comfort needs
- Who should help coordinate
- How flexible the idea may be

Share only information relevant to the request through appropriate channels and with the person's permission. You do not need to send an entire medical record to begin. The team can follow up if further information is needed.

Read [what information is needed to request an adult cancer wish](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/) for more detail. You can also learn [what happens after you request an adult cancer wish](/blog/what-happens-after-you-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/) so you know what to expect next.

## Privacy and Dignity Come First

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Families can discuss their preferences about photographs, personal details, and who participates. Receiving a Moment does not require turning a private experience into a public story. Our [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) provides more information about submitted information.

## Start With the Person and What Matters

You do not need to know whether every detail is possible before reaching out.

If you know an adult in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, start by telling us about the person and what would be meaningful.
