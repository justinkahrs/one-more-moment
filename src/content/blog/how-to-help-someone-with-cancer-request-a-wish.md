Many adults facing cancer do not have the energy, time, or emotional bandwidth to search for wish-granting support on their own. They may be managing appointments, symptoms, family responsibilities, hospice conversations, treatment decisions, or simply the weight of another hard day.

That is why help from someone nearby can matter. A spouse, adult child, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, chaplain, church, or community contact can often help start the conversation gently.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. A Moment may be simple, private, joyful, practical, or deeply personal. What matters most is that it reflects the person facing cancer and gives them meaningful time with loved ones.

Some people search for phrases like "wish for adults with cancer," "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer," "adult cancer wish request," or "wish granting for adults with cancer." One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit and is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. Our work focuses on adult cancer wishes in Michigan for people facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

For a broader overview of adult cancer wishes in Michigan, visit our guide to [wish granting for adults with cancer in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## Start With What Would Feel Meaningful

The best Moment begins by listening to the person facing cancer.

Before thinking about forms, logistics, donors, or details, start with a simple question: What would feel meaningful right now?

That answer may be different for every person. A meaningful Moment might include:

- A favorite place
- A meal with loved ones
- A family gathering
- A sporting event
- A short Michigan getaway
- Something brought to the person if travel is not possible

The goal is not to create the biggest possible experience. The goal is to create something that feels personal, dignified, and real. Sometimes the most meaningful request is a quiet dinner. Sometimes it is seeing Lake Michigan again. Sometimes it is a favorite team, a favorite restaurant, or time with people who have been part of the person's life for decades.

If you want to help someone with cancer request a wish, listening is the first act of care.

## Make Sure the Person Facing Cancer Is Comfortable

The person facing cancer should stay at the center of the request whenever possible. Their dignity, comfort, and preferences matter.

Helping someone request a Moment does not mean planning around them. It means helping create something meaningful for them.

Whenever possible, make sure the person understands that a request is being started and is comfortable with the idea. Ask how much they want to be involved, what information they are comfortable sharing, and whether they have privacy concerns.

Some people want to talk through every detail. Others may want a loved one, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, or social worker to help with communication because they are tired or overwhelmed. Both can be okay when the person's wishes and dignity guide the process.

One More Moment does not provide medical advice, medical care, or emergency services. Health-related questions should always be directed to the person's medical, oncology, hospice, or care team.

## Gather Helpful Information

You do not need to write a perfect request. You only need enough information to help One More Moment understand the person, the situation, and what kind of Moment may be meaningful.

Helpful information may include:

- Name and contact information
- Best contact person
- Relationship to the person facing cancer
- General diagnosis or eligibility information
- Whether the person is facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer
- Where the person lives
- What kind of Moment may be meaningful
- Who should be included
- Timing or urgency
- Comfort, mobility, travel, or privacy needs

This information helps One More Moment review the request thoughtfully and follow up with care. A request a wish for someone with cancer does not need every detail settled before the first conversation. It simply needs a clear starting point.

For more guidance, read [What Information Is Needed to Request an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## You Do Not Need to Have Every Detail Figured Out

Families can reach out even if the Moment is still just an idea.

Maybe you know someone wants to spend time at a favorite place but you are not sure how travel would work. Maybe a meal, visit, or gathering seems meaningful, but you do not know what is realistic. Maybe a caregiver request for an adult cancer wish starts with one simple sentence: "I think this person could use a meaningful day with the people they love."

That is enough to begin.

One More Moment can ask follow-up questions, learn more about the person, and consider what may be possible. Submitting a request starts a thoughtful review. It does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability, but it opens the door to a conversation.

## Why Timing Matters

Timing matters because cancer can change quickly.

Earlier requests are often easier to plan because they allow more time for travel, lodging, meals, accessible seating, family schedules, local partners, donor support, and business support. More time can also make it easier to listen well and adjust the plan around the person's comfort.

That does not mean it is too late to ask if timing feels urgent. It simply means that reaching out sooner can create more room to plan with care.

Health, energy, mobility, and comfort may change from week to week or even day to day. A hospice cancer wish, oncology team referral adult cancer wish, or family request may need to move quickly. Sharing timing and urgency honestly helps One More Moment understand what kind of response may be realistic.

## Who Can Help Start a Request?

Many different people can help begin the request process.

A request may be started or supported by:

- The person facing cancer
- Spouse or partner
- Adult child
- Parent
- Sibling
- Friend
- Caregiver
- Hospice worker
- Oncology team member
- Nurse navigator
- Social worker
- Chaplain
- Church or community contact

The person facing cancer should be involved whenever possible and appropriate. A loved one or professional can help with the form, gather information, make an introduction, or share the request with One More Moment when the person does not have the energy to manage it alone.

To learn more about specific referral situations, read [Can Caregivers Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/can-caregivers-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/) and [Can Hospice or Oncology Teams Refer Someone for an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/can-hospice-or-oncology-teams-refer-someone-for-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## What If the Wish Needs to Change?

Cancer can change plans quickly. A trip may become a meal. A favorite place may be brought to the person. A larger family plan may become something smaller and more private.

That does not make the Moment less meaningful.

One More Moment understands that comfort, mobility, timing, and energy can shift. A meaningful Moment should serve the person, not force them into a plan that no longer fits. If something changes, share that information as soon as possible so the request can be considered with the person's current needs in mind.

For examples of what Moments may look like, read [What Types of Wishes Can Adults With Cancer Request?](/blog/what-types-of-wishes-can-adults-with-cancer-request/).

## Dignity and Privacy Matter

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private.

A person or family should not feel pressured to turn a personal experience into a public story. Photos, names, details, and updates should be handled with care and respect. A private Moment can be just as meaningful as one that is shared with the wider community.

If privacy is important, say so when starting the request or during follow-up. You can also review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) before submitting personal or medical information.

## Every Request Is Reviewed Thoughtfully

Every request is reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity and every donor deserves to know their support is used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the person's situation, eligibility, mission fit, timing, comfort, logistics, available resources, and whether additional information may be needed. Some requests may require follow-up with the requester, family, referral partner, or care team before a decision can be made.

Public donations may help cover direct wish-granting expenses such as lodging, meals, travel, tickets, accessibility needs, or other costs connected to an approved Moment. Because corporate donors and generous families and supporters help cover overhead, **100% of public donations go directly toward wish granting.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I help someone with cancer request a wish?

Yes. A family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, chaplain, church, or community contact can often help someone with cancer request a wish. The person facing cancer should remain central to the request whenever possible.

### Does the person facing cancer need to be involved?

Whenever possible, yes. Their comfort, dignity, privacy, and preferences should guide the request. In some situations, a trusted loved one or professional may help with communication if the person is tired, overwhelmed, or unable to manage the details alone.

### What information is helpful when starting a request?

Helpful information includes the person's name and contact information, the best contact person, relationship to the person, general diagnosis or eligibility information, whether the person is facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, where they live, what kind of Moment may be meaningful, who should be included, timing or urgency, and comfort, mobility, travel, or privacy needs.

### Can caregivers, hospice workers, or oncology teams help?

Yes. A caregiver request adult cancer wish, hospice cancer wish referral, or oncology team referral adult cancer wish can help someone learn that support exists. Caregivers and professionals can help start the conversation, gather information, share the request form, or make an introduction.

### What if we are not sure what kind of Moment to request?

You can still reach out. The request can begin with an idea, a meaningful person or place, or a simple sense that someone could benefit from time with loved ones. You do not need to have every detail figured out before starting.

### How can someone request an adult cancer wish in Michigan?

Use the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).
