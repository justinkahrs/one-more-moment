Yes, an adult cancer wish may include a sporting event if it is meaningful, realistic, and possible to coordinate.

For some adults, a game is more than a game. It may represent a favorite team, a family tradition, childhood memories, time with a spouse or child, or something they have always wanted to experience. A sports wish for adults with cancer can be loud and joyful, but it can also be deeply personal.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. A Moment is not about the size of the event. It is about the meaning behind it, the people gathered together, and the dignity of honoring what matters to the person facing cancer.

This article explains how an adult cancer wish sporting event request may work, what factors matter, and what alternatives may be considered if attending in person is not possible. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit and does not provide medical advice or promise access to any team, player, ticket, or memorabilia.

For a broader overview of adult cancer wishes in Michigan, visit our guide to [wish granting for adults with cancer in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## Why a Sporting Event Can Be Meaningful

A sporting event can carry years of memories.

For one person, a Detroit Tigers game may bring back summers with a parent or grandparent. For another, a Detroit Lions game may be tied to Sundays with family, a favorite jersey, or decades of cheering with friends. A college game, local team, or community event may hold the same kind of meaning.

A sports-related Moment may offer:

- Favorite team memories
- Time with children, siblings, a spouse, friends, or caregivers
- A shared tradition
- A break from appointments and stress
- Something to look forward to

Cancer can make normal life feel interrupted. A game can give a person and family a chance to step back into something familiar, spirited, and human. The goal is not to make cancer disappear. The goal is to create space for connection.

## What Types of Sports Wishes Are Possible?

The right sports Moment depends on the person, timing, health, comfort, location, availability, and what can realistically be coordinated.

Possible examples may include:

- A Detroit Tigers game
- A Detroit Lions game
- A Detroit Pistons game
- A Detroit Red Wings game
- A Michigan State or University of Michigan game
- A local minor league or community sporting event
- A private watch party if attending in person is not possible

These examples are possibilities, not guarantees. A Detroit Lions wish for an adult with cancer or a Detroit Tigers wish for an adult with cancer may depend on the season, tickets, accessible seating, transportation, weather, timing, and available resources.

One More Moment does not imply any formal partnership with the Detroit Lions, Detroit Tigers, Detroit Pistons, Detroit Red Wings, Michigan State University, the University of Michigan, or any team unless such a relationship is specifically confirmed.

## Accessibility and Comfort Matter

Accessibility and comfort are central to planning a sports-related Moment.

Before a game-day wish can be considered, it may be important to think through:

- Seating needs
- Wheelchair access
- Transportation
- Timing
- Crowd size
- Weather
- Restroom access
- Whether a quieter alternative may be better

Some stadiums and arenas require more walking, waiting, noise, and energy than a person expects. Weather can also matter, especially for outdoor events. Even when a person wants to attend, the most caring plan may include backup options, shorter timing, accessible seating, or a quieter version of the experience.

One More Moment does not provide medical care, emergency services, or medical advice. Families should talk with their care team about health, mobility, comfort, and safety questions.

## What If Attending the Game Is Not Possible?

Sometimes attending a game in person is not realistic. Cancer can change plans quickly, and tickets, travel, crowds, weather, or comfort needs may make an in-person event difficult.

When needed, a Moment may be adapted. Possible alternatives may include:

- A watch party
- A signed item if available
- A video message if possible
- A team-themed meal or gathering
- A game-day experience at home

These options still depend on availability, timing, resources, and what can be coordinated. One More Moment cannot promise access to teams, players, tickets, signed items, video messages, or memorabilia.

An adapted Moment can still be meaningful. A living room full of family wearing team colors, a favorite meal before kickoff, or a quiet game-day gathering may better fit the person's comfort while still honoring what they love.

## Who Can Help Start the Request?

The person facing cancer may begin the request, but they do not have to handle the process alone.

A sports-related Moment request may be started by:

- The person facing cancer
- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A chaplain
- A church or community contact

The person facing cancer should remain central to the request. One More Moment wants to understand what team, game, tradition, person, or memory makes the sports wish meaningful to them.

To begin, visit [Request a Moment](/moments/#request-a-moment). If you are not sure whether someone qualifies, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## Why Timing Matters

Sports wishes often need advance planning because tickets, accessible seating, travel, health, weather, and schedules can all affect what is possible.

Earlier requests usually give more room to explore options, understand comfort needs, coordinate family schedules, consider transportation, and plan around game dates. Some requests may be urgent, and One More Moment understands that families do not always have much time. Still, when possible, starting earlier can make planning easier.

If the original plan has to change, that does not mean the Moment has failed. A game-day wish may shift from an in-person event to a private watch party, a team-themed gathering, or another experience that better fits the person's needs.

## Dignity, Privacy, and Public Sharing

Dignity and privacy matter in every Moment.

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private. A person and family do not need to turn a meaningful experience into a public story for it to count.

Families may also share privacy concerns during planning. A sports-related Moment might involve public spaces, photos, crowds, or social media attention, so it is important to understand what the person and family are comfortable with.

For more information about how personal information is handled, review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/).

## Thoughtful Review and Donor Stewardship

Each request is reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity, and every donor deserves to know their support is used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the meaning of the requested Moment, timing, feasibility, available resources, accessibility, safety, privacy, and mission fit. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, tickets, timing, or availability. It gives the team a chance to understand the person, the request, and what may be possible.

Because corporate donors and generous families and supporters help cover overhead, 100% of public donations go directly toward wish granting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can an adult cancer wish include a sporting event?

Yes. An adult cancer wish may include a sporting event if it is meaningful to the person, realistic to coordinate, and possible within One More Moment's mission, timing, resources, accessibility needs, and safety considerations.

### Can someone request a Detroit Lions or Detroit Tigers game?

Yes, someone may request a Detroit Lions game, Detroit Tigers game, or another meaningful sporting event. Approval is not guaranteed and depends on ticket availability, timing, transportation, accessibility, weather, health considerations, and available resources. One More Moment does not imply a formal partnership with any team unless confirmed.

### What if attending a game is not possible?

If attending in person is not possible, the Moment may be adapted when feasible. Alternatives may include a watch party, team-themed meal, game-day gathering at home, signed item if available, or video message if possible. Access to tickets, players, teams, messages, or memorabilia cannot be promised.

### Are accessible seats or wheelchair access considered?

Yes. Seating needs, wheelchair access, transportation, restroom access, crowd size, timing, weather, and comfort are all important considerations when reviewing a sports-related Moment request.

### Who can help request a sports-related Moment?

The person facing cancer, spouse or partner, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, chaplain, church contact, or community contact may help start the request.

### How can someone request an adult cancer wish in Michigan?

Start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment). If you have questions first, email [contactus@onemoremoment.org](mailto:contactus@onemoremoment.org).

## About One More Moment

**One More Moment** grants meaningful wish experiences for adults in Michigan who are facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer and who lack the resources to create that Moment on their own.

Every request is reviewed with care. Eligibility may include Michigan residency, a qualifying cancer diagnosis, financial or logistical need, feasibility, timing, and confirmation from an oncology provider or care-team contact.
