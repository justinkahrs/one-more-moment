No, not every adult cancer wish can be granted exactly as requested.

One More Moment works to create meaningful Moments for adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, but eligibility, health, timing, location, cost, accessibility, availability, and logistics can all affect what is possible.

When an original request is not possible, the goal is often to work with the family to explore another meaningful option. A Moment may look different than the first idea and still carry the same heart: time with loved ones, dignity, connection, and memory.

Families may search phrases like "does every adult cancer wish get granted," "adult cancer wish," "cancer wish for adults," "make a wish for adults with cancer," "adult wish granting," or "Stage 4 cancer wish" because they are trying to understand how adult wish programs work. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit and is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish.

For a broader overview, visit our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/). If you already know someone who may need support, you can start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).

## Eligibility Comes First

One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan who are facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

Before a Moment can be planned, the team may need to understand whether the request fits the organization's mission and eligibility guidelines. Medical confirmation or other eligibility information may be required. This helps One More Moment review each request carefully and responsibly.

If you are unsure whether someone may qualify, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, availability, or a specific outcome. It gives One More Moment the chance to learn about the person, the situation, and what kind of Moment may be meaningful.

## Some Requests May Fall Outside One More Moment's Scope

One More Moment primarily focuses on Moments that can realistically be coordinated within its mission, resources, geographic area, and available support.

That means some requests may need to be adjusted. Out-of-state travel, for example, is generally not part of the organization's normal Moment planning. A request that begins as a faraway trip may sometimes become a meaningful local experience, a shorter outing, a special gathering, or something brought closer to home.

This does not mean the original idea was wrong to share. Families should feel welcome to explain what would matter most. Understanding the heart of the request can help the team explore what may be possible.

## Professional Sports and Celebrity Requests Can Be Difficult

Some adult cancer wishes involve professional sporting events, premium tickets, player meetings, athlete appearances, signed memorabilia, celebrities, or public figures.

Those requests can be meaningful, especially when a team, athlete, performer, or public figure has been part of a person's life story. They can also be difficult to coordinate because One More Moment does not control schedules, access, appearances, ticket inventory, venue policies, travel needs, or partner availability.

When a specific sports or celebrity request is not possible, the team may still be able to explore a related Moment. For example, the request might become a family watch party, a team-themed experience, a local outing, or another way to honor what the person loves.

The goal is not to replace the meaning. The goal is to look for a realistic path toward connection and joy.

## Health, Timing, and Comfort Matter

An adult cancer wish should be shaped around the person's comfort, dignity, and ability to participate.

Health can change quickly for someone facing advanced cancer. Energy, mobility, treatment schedules, hospice support, pain, appetite, weather, travel ability, and rest needs may all affect what is realistic. A request that sounded possible one week may need to be adapted the next.

Sharing health, accessibility, and timing needs early can help One More Moment plan with care. A shorter experience, quieter setting, accessible entrance, flexible schedule, local destination, or at-home Moment may sometimes be the most loving version of the request.

Families should continue working with oncology, hospice, and other care teams for medical guidance and health-related decisions.

## Cost and Available Support Can Shape What Is Possible

One More Moment wants to create as many meaningful Moments as realistically possible. Like any nonprofit, the organization must also consider available resources, donor support, vendor costs, transportation, lodging, meals, tickets, accessibility needs, and the details of each request.

Some Moments are simple and close to home. Others require more coordination, funding, community partners, or time. Every request is reviewed with care for the person facing cancer, their loved ones, donors, volunteers, and the partners who may help make a Moment possible.

Being flexible about the shape of the Moment can help preserve what matters most while keeping the plan realistic.

## A Different Option Can Still Be Meaningful

If the original adult cancer wish cannot be fulfilled exactly as requested, One More Moment may be able to explore another option with the family.

For example:

- A long-distance trip may become a local overnight stay or day outing
- A professional sporting event may become a family watch party or team-inspired experience
- A large gathering may become a smaller private meal
- A public outing may become something brought to the home
- A specific unavailable date may become another meaningful time together

The most important part of a Moment is not always the size, distance, or public visibility of the request. Often, it is the chance to be together, feel seen, share joy, and create a memory that belongs to the family.

## How Families Can Help the Review Process

Families do not need to have every detail figured out before reaching out. A few clear details can help One More Moment understand the request and respond thoughtfully.

Helpful information may include:

- The person's name
- Their general diagnosis or cancer status
- Where they live in Michigan
- What kind of Moment would be meaningful
- Why the request matters to them
- Whether travel is possible
- Any timing concerns
- Accessibility, mobility, comfort, or privacy needs
- Who should help coordinate the request

If you want a more detailed guide, read [What Information Is Needed to Request an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## What One More Moment Can Promise

One More Moment cannot promise that every request will be approved, that every adult cancer wish will be granted, or that a Moment will happen exactly as originally imagined.

What One More Moment can promise is care. The team reviews requests thoughtfully, respects the dignity of the person facing cancer, and looks for meaningful possibilities when a request fits the mission and resources allow.

That care matters. Even when the answer is not the exact first idea, the conversation can still lead toward something personal, loving, and real.

## Privacy and Dignity

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Receiving a Moment does not require someone to share photos, personal details, or medical information publicly. Families should share personal and medical information carefully. One More Moment uses submitted information to review eligibility and coordinate requests. You can review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information.
