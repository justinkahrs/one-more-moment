Yes. Some adults with advanced cancer may be able to receive a meaningful wish or Moment through adult wish-granting organizations like One More Moment.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. These Moments are not medical care, and they are not a promise that every request can be approved. They are a way to create time, connection, and dignity when a person and family are carrying a lot.

If you are searching for whether adults with advanced cancer can get a wish, the short answer is: sometimes, yes. For people in Michigan, One More Moment may be able to help review an adult cancer wish request and see whether it fits the mission, timing, resources, and eligibility guidelines.

For a broader overview of adult cancer wishes in Michigan, visit our guide to [wish granting for adults with cancer in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## What Is an Adult Cancer Wish?

An adult cancer wish does not have to look one specific way. For One More Moment, the word we use is Moment, because the heart of the experience is not size or spectacle. It is meaningful time with the people who matter.

An adult cancer wish, advanced cancer wish, Stage IV cancer wish, or metastatic cancer wish might include:

- Dinner with loved ones
- A visit to a favorite place
- A short Michigan getaway
- A peaceful day near the water
- A family gathering
- A meaningful conversation
- Time with children, grandchildren, friends, or caregivers

Some Moments are quiet. Some take planning. Some involve travel, meals, lodging, or coordination with family schedules. Others are as simple as helping make one ordinary day feel less crowded by appointments, stress, and logistics.

## Why Advanced Cancer Wishes Matter

When someone is facing advanced, metastatic, or late-stage cancer, time can feel more limited. Families may want to create a meaningful memory while the person they love is still able to enjoy it. The person facing cancer may want something to look forward to that is not another appointment, test, bill, or hard conversation.

A Moment can offer connection, dignity, and time away from logistics. It can give a family room to be together as people, not only as patients, caregivers, schedulers, and decision-makers.

That does not mean a Moment removes the seriousness of cancer. It does not. But late-stage cancer support can include more than treatment plans and paperwork. Sometimes support means helping a person share a meal, sit near the water, take a short trip, or gather the people they love in one place.

## Does the Wish Have to Be Big?

No. A Moment does not have to be a large trip, expensive event, or public experience to matter.

A simple dinner, a visit to a favorite place, or a quiet day with family can be deeply meaningful. For some people, the most important adult cancer wish is not about going far away. It is about feeling present with loved ones, being remembered with dignity, or having one more peaceful day that feels like their own.

If you are wondering whether an idea is too small, it probably is not. The size of the request is not what gives a Moment meaning. The meaning comes from the person, the family, and the reason the Moment matters to them.

## Who Can Help Start a Request?

A request can begin in several ways. The person facing cancer may reach out directly, or someone close to them may help.

People who may help start a request include:

- The person facing cancer
- A spouse or partner
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A social worker
- A church or community contact

The person facing cancer should stay central to the request whenever possible. A Moment should reflect what matters to them, not what others assume they should want.

If you are ready to begin, use the [Request a Moment](/moments/#request-a-moment) form. You can also review [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/) before submitting.

## When Should Someone Request a Moment?

Earlier requests are often easier to plan. More time can help with coordination, privacy needs, family schedules, travel, meals, lodging, local support, and the practical details that make a Moment possible.

This is especially true if a request involves a short Michigan getaway, a gathering with several people, or special timing around treatment, hospice care, mobility needs, or caregiver availability.

Submitting earlier does not guarantee approval, funding, or timing. It simply gives the One More Moment team more room to understand the request and see what may be possible. For more guidance, read [When Should You Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/when-should-you-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/).

## Dignity and Privacy Matter

One More Moment handles requests with care. Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private.

Families should never feel pressured to turn a personal experience into a public story. Privacy can help people relax, be honest, and experience the Moment in a way that feels right for them. Personal and medical information should be shared carefully and only as needed for review and coordination.

You can review the organization's [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) to learn more about how submitted information is used.

## How Requests Are Reviewed

Each request is reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity, and every donor deserves to know their gift is being used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the person, the diagnosis, Michigan connection, timing, logistics, safety, resources, and whether the requested Moment fits the mission. A request may also require confirmation from an oncology provider or care-team contact.

Because each situation is personal, submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability. One More Moment does not provide medical advice, medical care, ambulance transportation, oxygen, medical equipment, nursing support, dialysis, or hospice care away from home.

Because corporate donors and generous families/supporters help cover overhead, 100% of public donations go directly toward wish granting. That trust matters. It helps donors support adult cancer wishes in Michigan with confidence, and it helps the team focus public gifts on meaningful Moments for adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can adults with advanced cancer get a wish?

Yes. Some adults with advanced cancer may be able to receive a meaningful wish or Moment through adult wish-granting organizations. One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

### Does someone have to have Stage IV cancer to request a Moment?

One More Moment supports adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer. If you are unsure whether a diagnosis fits, you can [contact One More Moment](mailto:contactus@onemoremoment.org) before submitting a request. Confirmation from an oncology provider or care-team contact may be requested.

### What can an adult cancer wish look like?

An adult cancer wish can be simple or more involved. A Moment might include dinner with loved ones, a visit to a favorite place, a short Michigan getaway, a peaceful day near the water, a family gathering, or time with children, grandchildren, friends, or caregivers.

### Does the wish have to be a large trip?

No. The wish does not have to be a large trip. A simple dinner, visit, quiet day, or meaningful conversation can matter deeply.

### Can hospice or oncology teams help start a request?

Yes. Hospice workers, oncology team members, social workers, caregivers, family members, friends, and community contacts may help start a request or referral. The person facing cancer should remain central to the Moment being requested.

### How can someone request a Moment in Michigan?

The best place to begin is the [Request a Moment](/moments/#request-a-moment) form. You can also review [who qualifies](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/) or email [contactus@onemoremoment.org](mailto:contactus@onemoremoment.org) with questions.
