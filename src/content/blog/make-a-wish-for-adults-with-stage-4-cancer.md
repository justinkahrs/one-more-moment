Families often search for "Make-A-Wish for adults with Stage 4 cancer" because Make-A-Wish is the wish-granting name they know. When someone they love is facing advanced cancer, they may be trying to find a way to create one meaningful experience, one gathering, one quiet trip, or one more moment together.

Make-A-Wish is widely known for helping children with critical illnesses. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love.

If your search began with "make a wish for adults with stage 4 cancer," "make a wish for adults with cancer," "adult Make-A-Wish," or "adult cancer wish," this guide explains what families should know and where to begin in Michigan.

For a broader overview, visit our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/). If you already know someone who may need support, you can start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).

## Is There a Make-A-Wish for Adults With Stage 4 Cancer?

There is not one single national answer for every adult cancer wish. Make-A-Wish focuses on children with critical illnesses, while adults are often supported by independent wish-granting nonprofits with their own missions, locations, eligibility guidelines, and review processes.

One More Moment serves adults in Michigan who are facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer. The organization focuses on meaningful Moments rather than a one-size-fits-all definition of a wish.

A Moment might be quiet, joyful, practical, simple, or deeply personal. It may happen near home, outdoors, with family, with close friends, or in a place that carries special meaning. What matters most is that the Moment reflects the person facing cancer and gives them time with the people they love.

## How One More Moment Helps Adults in Michigan

One More Moment helps adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones. The organization is Michigan-based and reviews each request with care.

Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability. It does give the One More Moment team the opportunity to understand the person, the cancer journey, the requested Moment, the timing, and the practical details that may affect what is possible.

For families, that first step can be a relief. The request does not need to be perfectly written. You do not need every logistical detail before reaching out. You can begin with what you know and let the team review the situation thoughtfully.

To understand the main eligibility considerations, visit [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## What Can an Adult Cancer Wish Look Like?

An adult cancer wish does not have to be large, public, or elaborate to matter. Many of the most meaningful Moments are centered on time together.

A Moment may include:

- A quiet family weekend away
- Time near the water
- A favorite meal shared with loved ones
- A small family gathering
- A sporting event
- A visit to a meaningful local place
- A peaceful outdoor experience
- Something brought to the home if travel is not possible

Some Moments involve travel or community partners. Others are close to home. Some are full of laughter and activity. Others are calm, private, and reflective.

The size of the wish does not determine its value. For many adults facing Stage 4 cancer, the most meaningful gift is protected time with the people they love.

For more examples, read [Adult Cancer Wish Ideas for Families Facing Stage IV Cancer](/blog/adult-cancer-wish-ideas-for-families-facing-stage-iv-cancer/) and [What Types of Wishes Can Adults With Cancer Request?](/blog/what-types-of-wishes-can-adults-with-cancer-request/).

## Who Can Start a Request?

The adult facing cancer can request a Moment directly, but they do not have to carry the process alone.

A request may begin with:

- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A parent or sibling
- A family member
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A church contact
- A community member

Whenever possible, the person facing cancer should remain central to the request. Their comfort, preferences, privacy, dignity, and consent matter.

If a caregiver or loved one is trying to help, [Can Caregivers Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/can-caregivers-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/) explains how that can begin.

## What Information Is Helpful?

You do not need to have everything figured out before asking. Still, a few details can help One More Moment review the request carefully.

Helpful information may include:

- The person's name and contact information
- Your relationship to the person, if you are asking on their behalf
- The diagnosis or general cancer status
- Whether the person is in Michigan
- What kind of Moment may be meaningful
- Whether travel is possible
- Any timing, mobility, health, or comfort concerns
- The best person to help coordinate next steps

If some details are unknown, that is okay. A request can begin with a simple question and grow from there.

For a more detailed checklist, read [What Information Is Needed to Request an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## Privacy, Dignity, and Care

One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given. Many remain private. Receiving a Moment does not require someone to share photos, personal details, or medical information publicly.

Families should share personal and medical information carefully. One More Moment uses submitted information to review eligibility and coordinate requests. You can review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is One More Moment affiliated with Make-A-Wish?

No. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit with its own mission, eligibility guidelines, review process, and focus on adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer.

### Does Make-A-Wish grant wishes for adults with Stage 4 cancer?

Make-A-Wish is widely known for helping children with critical illnesses. Adults facing Stage 4 cancer may be supported by independent adult wish-granting nonprofits, depending on the person's location, diagnosis, timing, and the organization's guidelines.

### Is there a wish program for adults with Stage 4 cancer in Michigan?

Yes. One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones. Each request is reviewed individually and approval is not guaranteed.

### What can an adult cancer wish include?

An adult cancer wish may include time near the water, a family gathering, a favorite meal, a quiet trip, a sporting event, a meaningful local experience, or something brought to the home if travel is not possible.

### Who can request an adult cancer wish?

The adult facing cancer may request a Moment directly, and a spouse, adult child, family member, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, social worker, church contact, or community member may help begin the request.

### What if I am not sure someone qualifies?

It is still okay to ask. One More Moment reviews each request with care. You can read [What If I'm Not Sure Someone Qualifies for an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-if-im-not-sure-someone-qualifies-for-an-adult-cancer-wish/) or begin with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).
