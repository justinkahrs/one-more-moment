Yes, an adult facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer may still be able to receive a meaningful Moment while actively receiving treatment.

Treatment status alone does not automatically mean someone should wait to reach out. A person can still have appointments, ongoing treatment, and questions about the future while hoping to spend meaningful time with the people they love.

One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer. Each request is reviewed individually, considering eligibility, need, available resources, timing, logistics, and what may be possible. Receiving treatment does not by itself determine eligibility or guarantee that a request can be fulfilled.

For an overview of our work, visit [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/).

## Why Families Sometimes Wait

Families often have understandable questions about when to ask for support. They may assume treatment must end first, that the person needs to be in hospice, or that a wish is only appropriate when someone is much sicker or nearing the end of life.

Those assumptions can make it difficult to start a conversation. Someone may feel that asking now is too early, even when there is already something they would love to do with their family.

A meaningful Moment does not have to wait for a particular milestone in care. If an adult in Michigan is facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, families can reach out to ask whether the situation fits One More Moment's mission.

If you found us by searching for "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer," please know that One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. We are an independent Michigan nonprofit with our own eligibility guidelines and request review process. You can read more about [who qualifies for a One More Moment wish](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## Why Reaching Out Earlier Can Sometimes Help

Starting the conversation earlier may give everyone more time to understand the person's hopes and explore a workable plan.

Depending on the person's circumstances, there may be more flexibility around dates, more options for travel, or an opportunity to plan while an outing fits their current energy and mobility. It may also be easier to coordinate loved ones, arrange accessibility needs, and consider a backup plan.

These are planning considerations, not predictions about someone's health. Every person's situation is different. Reaching out sooner does not mean a family must commit to a date or have every detail ready.

It can simply mean sharing what would matter and learning what may be possible.

## Treatment Schedules Still Matter

When planning an adult cancer wish while in treatment, the person's comfort and care schedule belong at the center of the conversation.

Appointments, side effects, rest needs, energy, mobility, and accessibility can all affect timing. Families can share any days or periods that tend to work better for them, along with how much flexibility they need. A shorter outing, a later start, a nearby location, or time to rest may make a plan easier to manage.

If you are wondering about a cancer wish during chemotherapy or another treatment, One More Moment can discuss the practical details of a request. Medical questions about activities, travel, or timing should be discussed with the person's care team. One More Moment does not provide medical advice or clinical care. A Moment should be planned around care, without stopping, delaying, or changing treatment to make it happen.

## What Kinds of Moments May Work During Treatment?

A Moment can reflect what the person enjoys and who they want beside them. It does not have to be a large trip or an elaborate event.

Possible ideas include:

- A local outing to a meaningful place
- A meal at a favorite restaurant
- A sporting event with loved ones
- A weekend away in Michigan
- Time near the water
- A family gathering
- A quiet experience at home

Some people want a change of scenery. Others want familiar surroundings, a favorite meal, or a few unhurried hours with family. The person's wishes should guide the idea, including how active, quiet, public, or private they want it to be.

These examples are possibilities, not promises. One More Moment cannot guarantee every request, specific experience, date, or travel arrangement. Read [Does Every Adult Cancer Wish Get Granted?](/blog/does-every-adult-cancer-wish-get-granted/) for more about how requests are reviewed.

## What If Health Changes During Planning?

Plans may need to change as a person's circumstances change. Families can let the team know if an outing no longer feels manageable or if timing becomes more urgent.

A larger outing may become something local. A travel idea may become an experience brought to the home when feasible. A bigger gathering may become a smaller visit that feels more comfortable.

Changing the details does not take away the meaning behind the request. The goal may still be time with a spouse, a shared memory with children, or a chance to enjoy something familiar together.

One More Moment will consider what can realistically be adapted, but available resources and timing still matter. For a request with limited time, read [What to Do When an Adult Cancer Wish Needs to Happen Quickly](/blog/what-to-do-when-an-adult-cancer-wish-needs-to-happen-quickly/).

## Does Someone Have to Be in Hospice?

No. Hospice is not a requirement for reaching out to One More Moment.

One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, including people who may still be actively receiving treatment. Families do not have to wait until hospice to start the conversation.

If someone is already receiving hospice care, a request may still be considered, with attention to comfort, timing, and feasibility. Our article [Can Adults in Hospice Receive a Cancer Wish?](/blog/can-adults-in-hospice-receive-a-cancer-wish/) explains more.

## Who Can Start the Request?

The person facing cancer can begin the request, and they do not have to manage the process alone.

A spouse, adult child, other family member, friend, or caregiver may help. An oncology team member, social worker, hospice worker, or church or community contact may also help start the conversation or make a referral.

Whoever reaches out, the person facing cancer should remain central to the request. Ask what would feel meaningful to them, who they want involved, and who they would like to help coordinate the details.

## Helpful Information to Share

You do not need a finished itinerary before contacting One More Moment. A clear starting point can help the team understand the request.

Helpful information includes:

- General diagnosis or cancer status
- Where the person lives in Michigan
- The current treatment schedule, if relevant to planning
- Mobility or accessibility needs
- What would feel meaningful and who should be included
- Who should help coordinate the request
- Timing concerns or a need for flexibility

Share personal and medical information carefully through the request process. Confirmation from an oncology provider may be requested as part of reviewing eligibility. The team can follow up if more information is needed.

## Privacy and Dignity

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

A meaningful experience does not need to become a public story. Families can discuss their preferences and boundaries, including whether they want photos or details shared. You can review our [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information about submitted information.

## Start the Conversation While Treatment Continues

If you know an adult in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, you do not have to wait until treatment ends or health declines further before starting the conversation.
