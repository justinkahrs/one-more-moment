Yes, caregivers can often help start the request process for an adult cancer wish or Moment.

Many adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer may not have the energy, time, or emotional bandwidth to search for support resources themselves. A caregiver, family member, friend, hospice worker, oncology team member, nurse navigator, social worker, or other trusted person may be the one looking for help, asking questions, and trying to understand what might be possible.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. A Moment may be simple, quiet, joyful, practical, or personal. What matters most is that it reflects the person facing cancer and gives them time with the people who matter most.

Some people search for phrases like "can caregivers request a wish for an adult with cancer," "caregiver request adult cancer wish," "wish for adults with cancer," or "Make-A-Wish for adults with cancer." One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit and is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. Our focus is helping adults in Michigan create meaningful Moments when Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer has made time, logistics, and resources feel especially precious.

## Why Caregivers Often Start the Conversation

Caregivers often see the need before anyone else. They may notice when someone is missing a person, a place, a meal, a tradition, or a simple experience that would bring comfort and connection.

Caregivers also tend to carry a lot of the practical details. They may be helping coordinate schedules, appointments, family communication, transportation, meals, medications, comfort needs, and day-to-day logistics. Because they are close to the situation, they may have a clear sense of what would feel meaningful, realistic, or comforting.

Sometimes the person facing cancer knows exactly what they want but does not have the energy to start the process. Sometimes they are unsure how to ask. Sometimes a caregiver is searching for cancer caregiver support on behalf of someone they love and discovers that wish granting for adults with cancer may be available in Michigan.

A caregiver request for an adult cancer wish does not need to be perfectly polished. It can begin with a simple message: "Someone I love is facing advanced cancer, and I think a meaningful Moment might help."

## The Person Facing Cancer Should Be Part of the Moment

Whenever possible, the Moment should reflect what the person facing cancer wants.

A caregiver can help begin the request, gather information, communicate with family, and think through logistics. But the person's comfort, dignity, preferences, and consent matter. The goal is not to plan something around the person. The goal is to help create something meaningful for them.

That may mean asking gentle questions:

- Who would you want there?
- Is there somewhere you would love to go?
- Would you rather keep this private?
- What would feel peaceful, fun, comforting, or meaningful?
- What would feel like too much right now?

Some people want a full family gathering. Others want a quiet dinner, a short visit, a day near the water, or time with only one or two people. A meaningful Moment should honor the person, not pressure them.

One More Moment does not provide medical advice, medical care, or emergency services. Families should continue working with their medical, hospice, oncology, and care teams for health-related decisions. A Moment request is about meaningful time, not medical treatment or medical outcomes.

## Who Else Can Help Request a Moment?

A Moment request may come from the person facing cancer or from someone close to them. People who may help start the conversation include:

- Spouse or partner
- Adult child
- Parent
- Sibling
- Friend
- Caregiver
- Hospice worker
- Oncology team member
- Nurse navigator
- Social worker
- Chaplain
- Church or community contact

The person facing cancer should remain at the center of the request whenever possible. A loved one or care professional can help open the door, but the Moment should still reflect the person's preferences, comfort, dignity, and privacy.

If a hospice or oncology team is involved, they may also help provide helpful context around timing, mobility, comfort needs, and whether the requested Moment is realistic. To learn more, read [Can Hospice or Oncology Teams Refer Someone for an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/can-hospice-or-oncology-teams-refer-someone-for-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## What Information Is Helpful?

A caregiver does not need to have every answer before reaching out. Still, a few details can help One More Moment understand the request and respond thoughtfully.

Helpful information may include:

- The person's name and contact information
- Caregiver or requester contact information
- General diagnosis or eligibility information
- Where the person lives
- What kind of Moment may be meaningful
- Timing or urgency
- Who should be involved
- Privacy needs or concerns
- Any mobility, comfort, or logistical considerations

If you are unsure whether someone may qualify, [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/) and [What Are the Eligibility Criteria for an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/eligibility-criteria-for-an-adult-cancer-wish/) explain the main considerations in plain language.

## What Can an Adult Cancer Wish Look Like?

An adult cancer wish does not have to be large, expensive, or public to matter. A Stage IV cancer wish, advanced cancer wish, or metastatic cancer wish may be centered on connection, comfort, memory-making, or time together.

A Moment may include:

- Dinner with loved ones
- A favorite restaurant
- A short Michigan getaway
- A visit to a meaningful place
- A family gathering
- A sporting event
- A peaceful day near the water
- Time with children, grandchildren, friends, or caregivers

Some Moments involve travel or local partners. Others are close to home. Some are full of energy and laughter. Others are quiet and tender. The size of the Moment does not decide its meaning.

If you are ready to begin, use the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment). You can also read [How to Request a Wish for an Adult With Stage 4 Cancer in Michigan](/blog/how-to-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-stage-4-cancer-in-michigan/) for a step-by-step overview.

## Why Earlier Requests Can Help

Earlier requests are often easier to plan because they allow more time for coordination, family schedules, travel, meals, lodging, comfort needs, privacy needs, and local support.

That does not mean every request needs to happen far in advance. Sometimes a family realizes late that a Moment may be needed. It is still worth reaching out. But when there is more time, One More Moment may have more room to understand the person's hopes, talk through practical details, and explore what may be possible.

Earlier conversations can also reduce pressure on caregivers. Instead of trying to solve every detail alone, they can share what they know and let One More Moment review the request with care.

For more on timing, read [When Should You Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/when-should-you-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/).

## Dignity, Privacy, and Personal Boundaries

One More Moment handles requests with care. Every person and family deserves dignity, privacy, and the ability to decide what feels right for them.

Some Moments are shared publicly only with permission. Many stay private. Receiving a Moment does not require someone to share photos, personal details, or medical information publicly.

Caregivers may also have privacy concerns of their own, especially when family communication is complicated or when medical information is sensitive. You can review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information about how submitted information is used.

## How Requests Are Reviewed

Each request is reviewed thoughtfully because every person and family deserves dignity and every donor deserves to know their support is used responsibly.

One More Moment considers the person's situation, the requested Moment, mission fit, timing, logistics, safety, available resources, and whether additional information may be needed. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability. It begins a careful review.

Public donations may help cover direct wish-granting expenses such as lodging, travel, meals, tickets, accessibility needs, or other costs connected to an approved Moment. Because corporate donors and generous families and supporters help cover overhead, **100% of public donations go directly toward wish granting.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can caregivers request a wish for an adult with cancer?

Yes. Caregivers can often help start the request process for an adult cancer wish or Moment. One More Moment supports adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, and caregivers may help share information, coordinate details, or ask questions on behalf of someone they love.

### Does the person facing cancer need to approve the request?

Whenever possible, yes. A caregiver can help begin a request, but the person's comfort, dignity, preferences, and consent matter. The Moment should reflect what feels meaningful and respectful to the person facing cancer.

### What information should a caregiver provide?

Helpful information includes the person's name and contact information, caregiver or requester contact information, general diagnosis or eligibility information, where the person lives, what kind of Moment may be meaningful, timing or urgency, who should be involved, privacy needs, and any mobility or comfort considerations.

### Can hospice or oncology teams help with a request?

Yes. Hospice workers, oncology teams, nurses, nurse navigators, social workers, chaplains, and other care professionals may help begin a request or referral. They may also help provide context around timing, comfort needs, mobility, and logistics.

### What can an adult cancer wish look like?

An adult cancer wish may be dinner with loved ones, a favorite restaurant, a short Michigan getaway, a meaningful visit, a family gathering, a sporting event, a peaceful day near the water, or time with children, grandchildren, friends, or caregivers.

### How can someone request an adult cancer wish in Michigan?

The best place to begin is the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment). You can also review eligibility information, read related planning guides, or contact One More Moment at [contactus@onemoremoment.org](mailto:contactus@onemoremoment.org) with questions.
