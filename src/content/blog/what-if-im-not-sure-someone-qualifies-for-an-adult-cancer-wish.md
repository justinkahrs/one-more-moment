If you are not sure whether someone qualifies for an adult cancer wish, it is still okay to reach out and ask.

Many families, caregivers, friends, hospice workers, oncology teams, social workers, church contacts, and community members are not sure where to begin. They may know someone facing cancer who could use one meaningful Moment with loved ones, but they may not know whether the person meets eligibility guidelines, whether the timing is right, or whether the request is the kind of thing a nonprofit can help with.

One More Moment helps adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with the people they love. Every request is reviewed with care. Asking a question or submitting a request does not have to mean you have every answer already.

For a broader overview, visit our guide to [adult cancer wishes in Michigan](/adult-cancer-wishes-michigan/). If you are ready to begin, you can start with the [Request a Moment form](/moments/#request-a-moment).

## Why People Hesitate Before Reaching Out

It is very common to pause before asking about an adult cancer wish. People often hesitate because they are unsure about:

- The exact diagnosis or stage
- Whether "advanced" or "metastatic" applies
- Whether the person lives in the right service area
- Whether there is enough time to plan something
- Whether travel is possible
- Whether the person is well enough for a Moment
- Whether the request is too simple
- Whether the request is too big
- Whether a caregiver, family member, friend, or professional can ask on the person's behalf

That hesitation is understandable. A cancer journey can involve a lot of changing information, and families are often trying to make decisions while also managing appointments, symptoms, schedules, emotions, and everyday life.

The request does not need to be perfectly worded. It can begin with a simple question: "I know someone in Michigan facing advanced cancer, and I am not sure whether they qualify. Can we ask?"

## What One More Moment Generally Looks For

One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan who are facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer. The purpose of a Moment is to help create meaningful time with loved ones when cost, timing, logistics, health, or available resources make that difficult to create alone.

Every request is reviewed individually and thoughtfully. Submitting a request does not guarantee approval, funding, timing, or availability, but it gives the One More Moment team a chance to understand the person, the situation, and what kind of Moment may be meaningful.

If you want to compare your situation with the main eligibility considerations, read [Who Qualifies for a One More Moment Wish?](/blog/who-qualifies-for-a-one-more-moment-wish/).

## What Information Is Helpful

You do not need to have every detail figured out before reaching out. Still, a few pieces of information can help One More Moment review the request with care.

Helpful information may include:

- The person's name and contact information
- Your name and contact information, if you are asking on their behalf
- The diagnosis or general cancer status
- Whether the person is in Michigan
- What kind of Moment may be meaningful
- Whether travel is possible
- Any timing, health, mobility, or comfort concerns
- The best family member, caregiver, or medical contact to help coordinate

If you are unsure about some of these details, that is okay. Sometimes the first step is simply starting the conversation so the team can understand what is known, what still needs to be clarified, and whether the request may fit the mission.

For more detail, you can also read [What Information Is Needed to Request an Adult Cancer Wish?](/blog/what-information-is-needed-to-request-an-adult-cancer-wish/).

## The Request Does Not Have to Be Perfect

Families sometimes wait because they feel they need the whole Moment planned before they ask. They may think they need exact dates, full guest lists, travel details, medical clearance, costs, transportation plans, and every family preference already settled.

In many cases, you can begin before all of that is final.

A request can start with what you know. One More Moment can review the information, ask follow-up questions, and help determine whether the request may be possible. The most important thing is that the Moment reflects the person facing cancer, respects their dignity and comfort, and creates meaningful time with people they love.

If a caregiver or loved one is helping, they can start the process too. Learn more in [Can Caregivers Request a Wish for an Adult With Cancer?](/blog/can-caregivers-request-a-wish-for-an-adult-with-cancer/).

## A Moment Does Not Have to Be Big

A meaningful adult cancer wish does not have to be a large trip or public event.

A Moment may be a favorite meal, a small family gathering, a sporting event, a weekend away, time outdoors, a visit to a meaningful place, or something brought to the home if travel is not possible.

The size of the Moment does not decide its meaning. What matters is whether it gives the person and their loved ones time, connection, peace, joy, or memory in a way that feels true to them.

## Who Can Ask About a Moment

The person facing cancer can request a Moment, but they do not have to carry the process alone. A request or question may begin with:

- A spouse or partner
- An adult child
- A parent or sibling
- A friend
- A caregiver
- A hospice worker
- An oncology team member
- A nurse navigator
- A social worker
- A church contact
- A community member

Whenever possible, the person facing cancer should remain central to the request. Their comfort, preferences, privacy, and consent matter.

One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. One More Moment is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.

If your search started with one of those phrases and you are looking for a wish program for adults with Stage 4 cancer in Michigan, One More Moment may be a helpful place to begin.

## Privacy and Dignity Matter

One More Moment respects the privacy and dignity of every warrior and family we serve.

Some Moments are shared publicly only when permission is given, while many remain private. Receiving a Moment does not require someone to share photos, personal details, or medical information publicly.

Personal and medical information should be shared carefully. You can review the [Privacy Policy](/privacy-policy/) for more information about how submitted information is used.

## When You Are Not Sure, Start With the Question

If you know an adult in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer and you are not sure whether they qualify, you can start by submitting a request or contacting One More Moment.

You do not need to know whether the Moment is "big enough." You do not need to know every logistical detail. You do not need to have the perfect words. If someone you care about may need one meaningful Moment with loved ones, it is okay to ask.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What if I am not sure someone qualifies for an adult cancer wish?

It is still okay to reach out and ask. One More Moment generally serves adults in Michigan facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer, and every request is reviewed with care.

### Does the request need to include medical records right away?

Not necessarily. Helpful information includes the diagnosis or general cancer status, but families do not need to have every detail ready before starting the conversation. One More Moment may ask for additional information if needed.

### Can a caregiver or friend ask on someone's behalf?

Yes. A spouse, adult child, friend, caregiver, hospice worker, oncology team member, social worker, church contact, or community member may help begin the request. The person facing cancer should remain central to the Moment whenever possible.

### Does an adult cancer wish have to involve travel?

No. Some Moments involve travel, but many can happen close to home. A meaningful Moment may be a meal, gathering, event, outdoor experience, or something brought to the home if travel is not possible.

### Is One More Moment affiliated with Make-A-Wish?

No. One More Moment is not affiliated with Make-A-Wish. It is an independent Michigan nonprofit helping adults facing Stage IV, advanced, or metastatic cancer create meaningful Moments with loved ones.
